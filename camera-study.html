One camera across the set?

About 54° above the board is the combined working estimate. The rook supplies the clearest constraint; the other original images are compatible with it. A practical bracket is roughly 50–58°, not a statistical confidence interval.

All measurements here use original 300px Lichess sprites. No generated candidates or reconstructed models determined this fit. Dashed back arcs are inferred, not visible evidence.

Independent fitting can select a very different angle while improving the visible arc by only a tiny fraction of one original pixel. These values are curve-fitting optima, not evidence of six different cameras.

Why most pieces cannot pin down the angle

Only part of a circle is visible on most collars. Moving the inferred center and changing its radius allows many complete ellipses to match the same short arc. Bevel highlights, shadows, rounded profiles and original raster blur add systematic uncertainty. Significant perspective would also require a fuller camera model.

The joint calculation gives each circle its own center and radius, but one shared minor/major ratio. The rook receives weight 1 and each partial arc 0.25. These are explicit reliability choices, not a statistical confidence model. Rounded outer collar silhouettes were excluded.
