# Where the hanging mane meets the neck

Below the ear, black candidate 2 and the black Lichess sprite both show a long, dark hollow. It begins under the ear, sweeps toward the back of the neck, and comes forward again at the bottom. In the current knight the flat, lighter face beside the mane fills much of this hollow. This is a separate shape from the sharpened upper shoulder.

At the registered 1,254-pixel reference camera I sampled the darkest part of the source hollow between image rows 460 and 640. The positions extracted independently from the two black sources agree closely. The green line in the drawing marks the first visible intersection between the modeled mane and the neck. The gold line follows the photographed hollow. Through rows 490–575 the green line sits a median 54 pixels to the right of the hollow in the black original and 55 in black candidate 2. Shadows have width. These distances locate a modeling discrepancy and do not assign a physical edge to an exact reference pixel.

Rays through the source hollow between y460 and y556 hit the current mane with no neck behind it. At y580, y604 and y628 the neck lies 0.72, 0.47 and 0.19 model units behind the mane along the camera ray. Narrowing the mane in isolation would open a gap. A sculptural approach is to bring the neck up under its edge, carve a concave turn where the two meet, and taper the separation as it approaches the base. Preserve the outer silhouette and the centered top of the mane. Inspect the join on both real flanks and from the back as well as at 54°.
