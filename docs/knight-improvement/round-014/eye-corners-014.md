# The sweep of the upper lid

The original black sprite shows a sloping eye tucked beneath the ear. Black candidate 2 resolves a continuous upper crease. It arches just above the bright central surface, then tapers into the nose. In the current carving the upper line runs nearly level on the ear side and meets a steeper line partway across. A pale triangular reflection draws attention to that junction. [Registered eye close-up](eye-opening-and-lid.png)

I traced the dark upper crease in each photograph. Compared with black candidate 2, the model’s crease sits about three pixels higher at the ear-side corner and three pixels lower near the muzzle. At the photographed ear-side position the model has a bright edge. The reference has a shadow. The brightest point near the center of the eye falls at (503, 480) in the model and (504, 473) in black candidate 2. The original supports the higher central reflection at its coarser resolution. [Grid without a drawn contour](eye-corner-grid.png) · [Trace measurements](upper-eye-sweep.json)

The green outline in the close-up comes from rays hitting the actual eye insert. It leaves room for a carved upper lip several pixels wide. The crease in the wood and the visible perimeter of the insert can follow separate curves. The side view will help decide how much depth to give that lip, how to carry it across the cheek, and how softly to merge the forward corner into the nose.

For the next eye comparison, look first at the expression in the whole horse. A shallow, swept almond opening with a coherent brow would fit this carving. Compare both profiles and turn it under a moving light. The eye should stay legible after the strongest reflection leaves its surface.
