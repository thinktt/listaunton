# Where the upper mane catches the light

Above the swept ear is a wedge of wood with a fine reflective edge. In both black photographs its upper face warms and brightens as it approaches the forehead. A small, irregular patch of light sits just above the front end of the edge. The current knight has a cool silver band across the middle and a comparatively dark front shoulder. [Registered crest close-up](crest-color.png) · [Light on the front shoulder](front-cap-glint.png)

The two references place the center of the strongest upper-plane light within three pixels of one another. The enlarged original places it at (435, 334), and black candidate 2 at (438, 333). The current knight places it at (380, 332). The central bevel is almost as bright as black candidate 2. Its color is much cooler. The difference between the red and blue channels on the traced center is 87 levels in black candidate 2 and 21 in the model.

I reopened the model and photographed the same area with the four mane lamps disabled one at a time. Removing the rear oval drops the center of the bright band from 159 to 49 on the 0–255 luminance scale. Removing the key, fill or upper strip individually changes that reading by less than three levels. The upper strip illuminates the broader cap. The real wood pigment and the shape underneath are visible in the final exposure with the glossy response suppressed. The edge near (420, 341) belongs to the mane. Farther forward at (465, 352) the camera meets the continuous head. The junction matters in profile and as the horse turns. A promising next comparison would give the upper bevel a warmer, finer roll into the forward point while keeping the darker underface and newly restored hanging mane.
