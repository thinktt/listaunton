# Bishop checkpoint 021 — remove the extra socket recess

The fixed-view audit continues from checkpoint 020. The external dimensions
are close, but the internal surfaces can still read as swollen rings. Tracing
the collars confirms that several apparent edges are glints or shadow troughs;
moving the entire piece or uniformly shrinking it would spoil the original
outline. This pass isolates a smaller, physical defect at the stem/socket join.

The former socket profile dipped outward from the stem and rose again before
rolling onto the socket wall. Vertical rays on the evaluated surface measure
an outward rise of about **0.567 original profile units** after the inner trough.
It produces an extra narrow ring in neutral shading. The reference shows a
simpler rolled shoulder, supported by the imagined side guide. The original's
low resolution does not precisely resolve this cross-section; this remains a
supported interpretation, not recovered source geometry.

The first test raises the inner profile. It removes the trough but moves the
stem's visible seating point and is rejected. The accepted alternative lowers
the inward crest, changing only the heights of controls **28, 29 and 30**.
The stem seating control, every radius, outer socket wall, flare, foot, collars,
head, button and slit remain exact. The maximum outward height reversal becomes
zero in the same evaluated-surface measurement. See `socket-meridian.png` and
the complete sampled rays in `geometry/`. This is a shape measurement, not a
new reference alignment or material adjustment.

A separate test lifts three lower-collar underside controls. It makes the
collar slightly shallower, but worsens its original-white row-edge error from
about **2.90 to 4.74 comparison pixels**. The fixed-view improvement is not
convincing, so that test is also rejected. It is not part of the accepted scene.
The earlier all-three-collar compression remains rejected as well.

Fixed wood and neutral views, close source crops, actual surface measurements
and a level view with broad unfitted lights are compared at unchanged framing.
The socket now flows into the stem without the added inward recess. Its real
width and height envelope stay fixed. The inherited pigment and local fitted
reflectors still contain mismatched photographic cues; the extra glints visible
in wood are not evidence that the geometry is complete. No new texture or lamp
fit is made in this geometry checkpoint.

The final saved/reopened 512-sample render has outline overlaps of **98.7425%**
against original white, **97.0048%** against selected white 3 and **97.7850%**
against original black. These are effectively unchanged from checkpoint 020:
original white decreases by roughly 0.0015 percentage points. The local surface
improvement, rather than an outline gain, justifies the acceptance. The selected
image is not used to overrule the original's larger shape relationships.

The working scores remain approximately **97.00 / 81 / 83 / 77 / 72**, mean
**82.00%**, with holistic resemblance around **70–80%**. The local improvement
is too small and the interior estimate too uncertain to justify raising that
score. Only silhouette is measured; other scores are subjective. Collar face
curvature, apparent lower-head fullness and the flare/socket relationship still
need careful separation from material and light. The geometry goal is active
and incomplete; the full material/lighting mission is not complete either.

UVs, source pixels, packed pigment, lamps and exact render receivers. Actual
