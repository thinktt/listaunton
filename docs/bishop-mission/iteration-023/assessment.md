# Bishop study 023 — base curvature and stem taper

Checkpoint **022 remains the accepted model**. This is a completed investigation
with five rejected geometry trials, not a new accepted reconstruction. The
public review, render, rotating mesh, materials and saved 54-degree camera are
unchanged. The geometry goal is active and incomplete.

The owner's blink recording exposes an apparent fullness that a high outer
silhouette overlap does not explain away. The recording was previously decoded
and recorded in iteration 019; it is not new evidence or a new source image.
This study follows up on the base and lower-body transitions. Original white
is the authoritative target; selected white 3 and the imagined side guide
support interpretation. Sources and registration remain unchanged.

## Base tests

The current middle flare has a nearly straight interval and a small inward
curvature change. Two radius-only proposals replace controls 15–19 with a
rounded meridian between unchanged controls 14 and 20. Both remove the inward
flex, but expand the rear shoulder in the fixed view. Original-white outline
IoU falls from 98.7425% to 98.6843% (gentle) and 98.6060% (round). The smoother
profile is not by itself enough to justify the new silhouette or stronger
inherited highlights. Neither is accepted.

A bounded local height fit restores the original outline and increases its
IoU to 98.7567%. The actual evaluated surface, however, develops a worse inward
flex: the maximum measured radius second derivative rises from about 0.0704
to 0.1366. Its nearly straight interval also increases. The level view confirms
the unwanted bend, so this higher-scoring version is rejected.

A second height family retains a smoothly rounded meridian without that
inward flex. It produces 98.7037% original-white IoU and no convincing visual
gain at the reference view. Much of the flare still appears nearly conical.
That proposal is also rejected. These figures diagnose this particular
cross-section; they are not proof of the unknown source model's curvature.

## Stem test

There is a small width discrepancy in the exposed region below the lowest
collar. At this elevated angle, lower portions of the stem generate parts of
that outline. An initial analytical check shows that changing the physically
uppermost controls 38–40 does not change this boundary; no Blender trial or
public edit is made from that zero-improvement proposal.

A regularized fit of radial controls 33–36 narrows the middle of the taper and
widens its lower portion. Actual outline overlap improves against all three
images: 98.7733% original white, 97.0167% selected white and 97.8191% original
black. Nevertheless, a level render shows an unwanted lower-stem bulge and
abrupt transition. The fixed-view crops also reveal the extra swelling near
the socket. This version is rejected rather than accepted for its scores.

## Evidence and next work

Each geometry trial was saved and reopened, with its source hash, exact edit
declaration, render-receiver check and unchanged material/light/camera/UV
snapshot recorded. Original-registration wood and neutral renders, measured
surface curves, actual outline reports and identical-camera level views are
preserved here. The full acceptance/export/page pipeline is deliberately not
run for rejected models. No native browser QA is claimed.

The accepted checkpoint's working scores stay unchanged: approximately
97.00 outline / 82 interior form / 83 color / 77 texture / 72 lighting.
Only outline is measured; the other scores are subjective estimates, not
calibrated recovery percentages. Neither this study nor those figures resolve
the owner's whole-image concern. The retained pigment and reflection pattern
still disagree with the reference and can emphasize shape differences.

The next useful test should constrain a smooth stem meridian and identify the
actual surface points generating each visible contour. Avoid unconstrained
per-ring fitting that improves a narrow projection while adding a side-view
bulge. Continue checking internal edges and curved surfaces independently of
the photographic grain and highlights. No mission changes, quota monitoring,
workers, publication or changes to other pieces were made.
