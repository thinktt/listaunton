Looking at Staunton queens

A visual study of queens and their families, starting with the Lichess piece we are reconstructing. These examples are clues to a design tradition, not identified sources for the Lichess artwork.

A promising lead: Chavet. Several French Chavet sets have a flared queen’s crown with a low central finial, a button-topped bishop, and related turned bases. Their knights are visibly different from the Lichess knight. We have found no evidence that a Chavet set was the artist’s source.

Lardy and Chavet: Both makers were based in Dortan in France’s Jura region, part of a local woodturning tradition. Read the Chess Museum’s history of the region ↗

The photographs have different viewpoints and scales. They are loaded from their original websites; follow each source link for the complete image and context. Collector and seller descriptions identify their examples and are not independent proof of dates or makers.
